The challenge

Following a transition to a new transportation management system (TMS), Safran needed a way to evaluate shipping and transportation providers using the data available in the new platform.

My contribution

I interviewed members of the shipping team to understand which performance measures mattered to them. I selected the KPIs, learned how to extract the relevant TMS data, and mapped those measures to the available fields.

I then coded Excel macros to process an imported TMS data sheet, extract the selected KPIs, and display the results alongside an overall provider grade.

The deliverable

A reusable scorecard workflow: import the TMS export, press a button, and review the provider’s performance measures and grade.

My role: Industry research, survey and qualitative data collection, and supporting Python development.

Download illustrated report (PDF)

Read the case study

The challenge

Our client faced disintermediation: suppliers were beginning to sell directly to consumers, challenging the value of an intermediary in corporate gifting and personalization.

My contribution

I researched the corporate gifting industry, including competitors’ offerings, bundling models, and suppliers’ direct-to-consumer strategies. This work helped the team assess how a distributor could continue to add value.

I was responsible for the customer survey and qualitative data collection. The questionnaire explored gifting frequency, budgets, bundle preferences, online ordering, customization, and interest in recurring gifts. I also contributed to portions of the Python code in a supporting role.

What the research added

Customer and sponsor input helped connect the solution to purchasing preferences and operational constraints. The survey was exploratory: the small response sample could not establish market demand, so further customer research and a pilot remained important next steps.

Our team’s solution

We developed tools to support the client’s multi-brand bundling strategy and make a manual bundle-selection process more systematic. An Excel model supports scenario analysis and examination of brand and customer concentration. A Python risk engine uses historical revenue to calculate brand risk scores.

A second Python tool matches those scores to inventory, evaluates product combinations against user-selected constraints, and ranks qualifying bundles using weighted risk and margin criteria.

What the tools deliver

  • Brand risk scores and low, moderate, or high risk groupings.
  • Configurable bundle price, item count, category, brand, and margin filters.
  • Ranked bundle recommendations exported to Excel.

Team testing and outcomes

The final report describes bundle-list generation moving from a manual process measured in hours to an automated process measured in minutes. In the team’s comparison using matching input parameters, average proposed-bundle margin increased from 22.3% to 33.8%—an 11.5 percentage-point difference.

These were project testing results, not realized sales or profit. The delivered toolkit uses exported data and requires human review of product compatibility, packaging, brand restrictions, and customer appeal. It was not a live e-commerce or ERP integration.

Deliverables

An Excel risk model, two Python notebooks, a user guide, and a final report documenting the research, methodology, testing, and implementation considerations.
